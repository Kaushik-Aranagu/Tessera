In Tessera design work, Kaushik rejects on sight anything that reads as a drawing trying to look real: a canvas brass orrery, a cat silhouette, a cartoon woodland, worn paths, a sink. His words (Oct 25, 2026): "If they're drawn things, you should not aim too much. It clearly looks like a drawn thing. These are the kind of things I'm rejecting."

**Why:** Four Routines keystone rounds failed for this reason, not the concepts. What he approved had real material (photographed sky plates under glass, chalk texture on slate, paper tickets) or was deliberately graphic (pixel mosaic, typographic word wall).
**How to apply:** Decide a concept in words first. Then either (a) get real material: photographed or generated plates of the object with moving parts separated, and put the live layer on top; or (b) make it unmistakably graphic (type, hairlines, pixels, chalk marks). Never ship a procedural illustration as the look. See [[tessera-skypiece]], [[tessera-keystone-slot]].

**Correction (Oct 25, later):** "I don't need realistic images... the garden we previously had works, but a little bit improved, more finished, smoother. That is all that was needed, not realism." And "it's about making it functionally creative, functionally fun." The real rule: FINISH and FUNCTION. Drawn is fine when polished; half-finished sketches and photoreal comps both missed. Lead with what the keystone does (tending, consequences, visitors), then make it smooth.
