# 281 Names for every room (Oct 25; his pick pending)

His ask: "Rename all the apps. Cool, funny, witty, clever names for all of them."
Rules I held: one word each; a double meaning that lands on the room's keystone or its job; no Apple app names
(Notes, Pages, Reminders, Journal, Weather, Fitness, Wallet, Home); reads well in Tess's mouth ("in Chalk", "your
Habitat"); works as a door word at 24 pt; a set that sounds like one family. Tessera stays the app's name (a tessera is
one tile of a mosaic; Home is the mosaic).

## Set A: double meanings (recommended)
| Room | Name | Why it is clever |
|---|---|---|
| Home | **Mosaic** | It is the mosaic; Tessera is one tile of it. The app's name explains the home screen. |
| Notes + Lists | **Scratch** | Scratch notes, and you scratch things off a list. One word covers both kinds. (Collision: MIT's Scratch exists on the App Store; a fallback is **Jot**.) |
| Weather | **Glass** | The weather glass is the old word for a barometer ("the glass is falling"), and the skypiece is a glass vessel with the real sky in it. |
| Training | **Chalk** | Lifters chalk their hands; the keystone is chalk on a blackboard. Two kinds of chalk, one room. |
| Kitchen | **Pass** | The pass is where plates leave a kitchen; the keystone is the pass rail of tickets. Also "pass the salt". |
| Routines | **Habitat** | Where habits live: the woodland. Habit + habitat, and the woodland grows or wilts with them. |
| Money | **Means** | "Live within your means." The index chart is exactly that question, in and out against each other. |

Tess, in this set: "Your Habitat needs water." "Means closes the month at 179." "There's a list in Scratch for the
trip." "The Glass says rain by four." "Chalk is up: 80 for five." "Three tickets on the Pass."

## Set B: short verbs (what you do there)
Home **Look** · Notes **Jot** · Weather **Glance** · Training **Lift** · Kitchen **Cook** · Routines **Tend** · Money **Spend** (or **Keep**).
Plain and quick; "Tend" is good for the woodland, "Keep" is good for money ("for keeps"). Less witty than A, very
easy to say. Weakest: Home as Look.

## Set C: the keystone objects
Home **Mosaic** · Notes **Wall** · Weather **Sky** · Training **Slate** · Kitchen **Pass** · Routines **Grove** · Money **Ticker**.
Honest and visual; "Slate" has a second meaning (clean slate) and "Ticker" two (stock ticker, your heart). "Wall" is
flat for notes; "Sky" is generic.

## Mixing
The sets mix freely where one word is clearly better: A with **Grove** instead of Habitat if Habitat feels long;
A with **Slate** instead of Chalk if Chalk reads as a gym-only word; A with **Jot** if Scratch's collision matters.

## What renaming touches
The Home mosaic faces and tile labels, every room title (40 pt, room colour), the doors that name rooms ("From another
room" in Routines becomes "From another room" still, listing the new names), Tess's grammar (room words in phrases and
feedback lines), the handoff and specs (a rename table in design.md), and the App Store screenshots later. Colours and
keystones do not change.

## v3 (his note, Oct 25): "Find a theme: a language, a show, pop culture, something to take references from, and keep
it sensible. That's the primary thing."

### Theme 1 (recommended): the rooms of a Roman house
Tessera is the Latin word for a mosaic tile, and mosaics were the floors of Roman houses. So the app is a house, and
each app is the room of a Roman house that did that job. Every name is a real room, and each one is sensible once you
hear what it was for. Shown with an English line under the title the first few times.
| App | Name | The room | Why it fits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Home | **Atrium** | the central hall every room opens off | the place you arrive and see the whole house |
| Notes + Lists | **Tablinum** | the study where records and writings were kept | where your notes and lists live |
| Weather | **Impluvium** | the pool under the opening in the atrium roof that caught the rain | the one room that literally shows the sky |
| Training | **Palaestra** | the exercise court | where you train |
| Kitchen | **Culina** | the kitchen (and the root of "culinary") | what you cook |
| Routines | **Lararium** | the household shrine where the daily rituals were kept | the things you do every day |
| Money | **Arca** | the strongbox in the atrium | what you have and where it goes |
Short forms Tess can use: Atrium, Tablet (for Tablinum), Rain (for Impluvium), Palaestra, Culina, Lares, Arca.
Risk: Tablinum, Impluvium and Lararium are long and unfamiliar; mitigation is the subtitle and the short forms.

### Theme 2: Greek gods (pop-myth, instantly readable)
| Home **Hestia** (the hearth, the centre of the home) | Notes **Mnemo** (Mnemosyne, memory, mother of the Muses) | Weather **Zephyr** (the west wind) | Training **Nike** (victory; the brand collision is a problem, fallback **Hercules**) | Kitchen **Demeter** (harvest and grain) | Routines **Horae** (the goddesses of the hours and seasons, keepers of time's order) | Money **Plutus** (wealth) |
Readable, playful, a little grand. Horae for routines is the cleverest; Nike is the problem.

### Theme 3: a ship (sensible and plain-English)
| Home **Bridge** | Notes **Log** (the captain's log) | Weather **Barometer** or **Lookout** | Training **Deck** (drills on deck) | Kitchen **Galley** | Routines **Watch** (the watches that structure a ship's day) | Money **Purser** (the officer who keeps the money) |
Everyone gets every name at once; Watch and Purser are the clever ones. Less tied to Tessera.

Recommendation: Theme 1. It is the only theme that grows out of the app's own name, so the whole product tells one
story (a house of tiles); every name is real and means exactly the room's job.
