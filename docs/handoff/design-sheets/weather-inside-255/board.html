The four directions

1. Plain numbers, alive.Your first instinct: the temperature and a few small numbers. Each number behaves like its quantity. The wind number trembles with the gusts. The humidity number sweats: on damp days tiny beads of condensation form on the inside of the glass and run down. UV warms toward amber as it climbs. The sunset time counts down and glows at the end.
2. Tess says the weather.One line in her voice, Newsreader italic, next to the temperature: "Rain in forty minutes. The umbrella is by the door." Words arrive one at a time, as in her corner. The line rewrites itself when something changes, and it knows your day: the walk, the balcony plants, the gym at 6:30.
3. The number is a dial.The temperature is now, and you can turn it: swipe across the glass and it rolls through the next hours, 64, 63, 61, 59, with the hour and one word under it changing (Cloudy, Cloudy, Rain). Left alone it peeks at the next change every ten seconds and returns to now.
4. The sun’s day.One hairline across the glass is the sun’s path today, sunrise to sunset, with a bright point where it is right now and a moon point on its own path. The temperature sits under it. The line warms to amber near sunset, and rain hours are marked by a dotted stretch. The same grammar as a routine’s life line.
5. The skypiece (your idea).The glass is an open vessel with depth, and the weather gets in. Rain beads on it and runs down to collect as water along the bottom, with a surface that ripples where drops land and leans when you tilt the glass; in sun it slowly evaporates. Snow falls inside and piles in drifts, deeper in the corners, and melts when it warms. A lightning strike leaves a scorch mark with a few cracks that fades over the day. Wind shakes everything inside. On special days Tess dresses it: a string of small warm lights along the bottom, paper snowflakes turning. The numbers live in it, above the water line.
The glass has its own weather (all five).Because it is glass: on rainy or foggy days condensation beads and runs on the inside; in a cold snap frost grows from the corners and melts when it warms; in bright sun a soft flare slides across now and then. Always subtle, always inside the approved surface, never touching legibility.
Reactions (all four).Rain starts: words and numbers update within three seconds and the glass beads. Wind gusts: the wind number shakes. Cold snap: frost. Sunset: the light on the edge warms. Everything under five seconds; under Reduce Motion only the information changes. The skypiece’s accumulations (water, snow, scorch) are state, not effects: they stay as long as the weather says so.
