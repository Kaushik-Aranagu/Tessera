What changed from 248

Stacks show what is inside.The closed stack carries its count, one line of how it stands ("two to tend, one needs care") and a sheaf of hairlines in state colors. No counters, no bars: the hairlines are the sheets themselves seen edge on.
Three ways to stack.By grove (Body, Mind, Home, Across the lake), by how it stands (Needs care, To tend, Fruit, Done today) and by time (Morning, Afternoon, Evening, Any time). Sideways on the top line turns them. Sheets keep their identity and travel to their new stack in 0.5 s.
The pull follows your finger.Dragging a stack header fans the sheets live, in proportion to the pull. Let go past a third and it opens; short of that it settles back. Pull up on an open stack to close it. Tap does the same in one move.
A fanned sheet is a routine row.Name, one state word, and its time. Tap opens it; swipe right is Done with a short petal bloom and a strike; swipe left is Not now. Undo for 10 s. The bottom word follows the last sheet touched.
A routine leads with itself.Fixed block: name, then the whole life on one hairline (only the current stage is named). Below, three places on the dimension line: Steps, How it's going, About. Steps is where it opens.
One thing or many steps.A run of steps shows the current step large in plum with its how-to; done steps drop under a slate "Done" with a strike. A one-thing routine is one sentence of what to do, with its how-to under it, and checks off in one swipe.
How it's going is a time ribbon.Four weeks left to right, the day top to bottom. Each kept day is a short plum stroke at the time it happened, as long as it took. Drift, length and gaps read at once. A rest day is a slate tick at the foot. One Tess sentence says what it shows.
Pull the ribbon apart.The pull-apart move from the Codex studies: drag the ribbon down and it splits into one ribbon per step, so the step that drops first is plain to see. A one-thing routine splits by weekday instead.

Open, with a recommendation

What goes on About?

Shown: when it happens and on which days, how long it takes, its grove, where it comes from, when it was planted, its size ceiling and who can see it. Recommendation: keep these seven, and make When, Days and Size editable in place with a wheel, so there is no separate edit page.

Does the ribbon replace the seven strokes everywhere?

The strokes still appear on the 248 wheel and in the Habits grammar. Recommendation: the ribbon lives only on the routine page; rows elsewhere show one state word and no strokes, so the sheaf stays calm.

Steps inside a step?

A routine can hold a routine (Evening stretch inside Wind down). Recommendation: allow one level only, shown as a step whose how-to line names its own steps, which opens that routine on tap.
