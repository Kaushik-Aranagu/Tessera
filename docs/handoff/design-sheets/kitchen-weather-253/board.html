The sun is where it really is and as bright as it is; at night the moon rises and sets on its real arc in its real phase; clouds come when it is cloudy, rain falls, a storm flickers, fog softens everything. How it works

The pull.Letters spread from the middle, 30 pt per step, and lift 10 pt, over 0.6 s on a spring. The word shrinks from 52 to 40 pt as it opens so the inside has room. The other sections slide to make the gap (0.55 s) and dim to 40%.
Inside a section.Tonight: the dish, time, and its ingredients with missing ones in wine. Pantry: the five places with counts. Recipes: what you can make now. Groceries: what to get. Leftovers: what goes out when. Each is the first screen of that section, in place; its foot word changes with it.
The sun.Its height follows the real rise (7:25) and set (6:06). Brightness and the sky gradient follow its altitude: pale gold at dawn, white at noon, amber at dusk, slate below the horizon. Clouds cover it when the sky is overcast.
The moon.Rises and sets on its own arc (today 3:48 pm to 2:12 am) in its real phase (waxing gibbous), drawn as a lit disc with a shadow disc offset by the phase. Stars appear as the sun goes down.
Weather on the page.Cloudy: layered soft clouds drift. Rain: streaks fall the whole height, heavier below the clouds. Storm: a flash every few seconds lights the clouds from inside. Fog: a moving haze lowers contrast. Snow: flakes drift. Each is the same condition language as the Home weather words.
Words on the sky.Text color is computed from the sky’s brightness behind it: ink on bright skies, warm white at night and in storms. Every line keeps 4.5:1.
Care.Nudges from the forecast and the other rooms: take the umbrella at seven; close the windows before the storm; water the balcony plants, no rain till Thursday; sunscreen at noon, UV 7; frost tonight, bring the basil in. Swipe right when done; Undo 10 s.
