Loops, as mechanisms.

You defined it: a routine is a loop, a procedure that repeats on a period, daily or weekly, sometimes automated. None of the earlier rounds showed a loop, let alone loops at different speeds. The objects that embody loops and procedures are mechanisms. Two of them, each a real thing, each able to run by itself (your automated routines) or wait for your hand, each with Tess winding and writing.

The orrery. A tabletop orrery in brass. The center is now. Each routine is a small body on its own arm, orbiting at its own period: the daily ones on the inner ring go round once a day, the weekly ones (swim on Thursday, badminton on Saturday) on the outer ring go round once a week, and a monthly thing would sit further out still. A body comes to the front, under the pointer, when it is due. Done, it turns gold and polished for the rest of its orbit; passed and not done, it goes dark until it comes round again, and that is all. Steps are moons around a body. Automated routines turn by clockwork and chime when they fire; yours wait for your hand. The whole thing turns in real time, slowly, and you can spin it ahead with a finger to see what is coming.

The music box. A brass drum turning once a day, with a comb of teeth beneath it. Each routine is a track along the drum, its name engraved; each step is a pin at its time. As the drum turns, a pin that reaches the comb plucks a tooth and a note rings, and the pin is polished: done. A step you skip passes the comb silent and dull. A routine with five steps is a short phrase of five notes; a one-thing routine is a single note. The weekly drum is a second, slower drum behind. Automated routines are pins the mechanism plays by itself; yours wait, and you pluck them with a tap. Tess winds the key. The procedure is literally written on the thing.

How they work

Loops at their own speed.Both mechanisms turn in real time. The orrery’s inner arms make one turn a day, the outer one turn a week; the box’s drum makes one turn a day with a slower weekly drum behind. Nothing is a gauge: an orrery and a drum are the real objects, and the turning is the point.
Procedure visible.Steps are moons (orrery) or pins (box). A five-step routine reads as five, at its times; a one-thing routine as one. Done steps are polished; skipped ones go dull until the next turn.
Automation built in.Your design decision for later has a home: automated routines are clockwork, driven by the mechanism and chiming when they fire; the ones you do yourself wait for your hand (a tap, a swipe). Tess winds the key, so the AI layer’s job is literal.
Kind to a miss.A missed routine simply passes and comes round again; it goes dark or dull, never red, and the next turn is a clean start. No streak is counted out loud. A planned rest is a body or pin set aside, marked by a small ring.
Information.What is next and when (the body or pin nearest the pointer), how full the day is (how many bodies or pins), what is weekly (the outer ring or the second drum), what is done. Tap a body or pin to open it.
Through the app.The mechanism stays in the slot on every Routines page. Behind a door it shows only that loop (Daily, Weekly) or that grove; on a routine’s page it shows that one orbit, or that one track, large, with its steps.

Where I would start

The orrery if the thing Routines should put in your face is when things come round: it is the clearest picture of daily and weekly loops side by side, and brass turning slowly is as calm and alive as the skypiece. The music box if the thing is the procedure: the steps are literally pins, and playing them is the most satisfying way I know to do a routine. They could be one object: an orrery whose bodies, when they come round, play their pins.
