The cat. A silhouette with eyes, so it stays graphic and never cartoonish. It has moods and places: the sun patch moves with the real hour; the door is at the right; the bowl at the left. It blinks, its tail has a life of its own, its ears turn toward sounds (a new ticket, a tap). What you do changes it: finish something and it purrs and slow-blinks at you; finish the walk and it comes back from the door and settles; leave a routine a few days and it sits facing away, looking over its shoulder now and then; miss a lot and it curls up small. Tap it to see what it thinks; stroke it (drag) and it leans into your finger. Under the slot, one line in Tess’s voice says what the cat would say.

How it works

Your routines are its routine.Each routine has a time, and the cat has a behavior for it: morning things = waking and the sun patch; stretch = the long stretch; walk = waiting at the door, then back; dinner = pacing by the bowl; evening things = the loaf; night = curled and asleep. Routines without a time (breaks, water) show as small habits: a yawn, a wash, a look at you.
It reacts to what you do.Done: a purr (a soft vibration you can feel) and a slow blink. Done late: it stands and comes to you. Not now: it flicks an ear and goes back to what it was doing. Three or more days left on one routine: it sits facing away and glances back at you. A week of nothing: it curls up small and sleeps more. Everything done today: it rolls onto its back in the sun.
It has a life when you are not acting.Blinks every 3 to 7 s, a double blink one time in five. The tail is six joints on a spring that follow the body, with a tip twitch when alert. Ears swivel toward a sound. Every few minutes it changes place: sun patch, windowsill, the door. It breathes. At night it dreams (paws twitch).
Tess writes its lines.One sentence under the slot, in her voice as the cat’s voice: "The sun is on this side of the kitchen until four. Stretch, then we both lie down." "You said walk at six. It is six." "Fine. I will look at the wall." The AI layer can own this entirely: the cat’s state is a small vocabulary (place, posture, mood, attention), and Tess writes to it.
Through the app.Behind a door the cat comes along: in Body it is stretching, in Mind it sits by the bookshelf, in Home it is on the windowsill. On a routine’s page it does that routine’s thing, small, while the steps are below.
The relation.If you have or had a cat, it can be that cat: its name, its color (the silhouette tints), its one habit (sleeps on the laundry; sits in boxes). If not, Tess names it and it becomes yours over the weeks. The cherry on top you asked for.

Open, with a recommendation

Which animal?

A cat is the strongest: it has a routine of its own that people recognize, it can be indifferent as well as affectionate (so a missed day reads as a look, never as a wound), and its silhouette is unmistakable at 180 pt. A dog is warmer but reads as needy when things slip. A bird (a parakeet on a perch) is the lightest and cheekiest. Recommendation: the cat, with the bird as the alternative if you want something that talks more.

How much face?

Eyes only, as here, keeps it graphic and lets the silhouette carry the acting. A mouth would push it into cartoon. Recommendation: eyes, ears, tail, posture. That is a lot of acting.
