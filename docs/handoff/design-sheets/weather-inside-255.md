# 255 Weather: what lives inside the glass (v1, Oct 25; not yet reviewed)

Board: https://claude.ai/artifact/XDNb4wm84nHNguDhFMqesr (file `weather-inside-255/board.html`). Builds on 254 v9 (the glass surface,
approved). His brief: inside the glass there should be life, personality, information worth showing, and reactions.

Four directions, each live inside the same approved glass on the same sky, chosen with a bar under the phone; hour
and sky controls as on 254; event buttons: Rain starts, Wind gusts, Cold snap, Sunset, Clears up.
1. Plain numbers, alive: temperature (Outfit 200 112 pt), the word for now with feels-like and high, and four small
   numbers along the bottom that behave like their quantity: wind trembles with gusts (and shakes on a gust event),
   humidity drifts and makes the glass sweat, UV turns amber at 6 and above, the countdown to sunset ticks and turns
   amber in the last hour.
2. Tess says it: the temperature at 84 pt and one line in Newsreader italic 19 pt beside it, arriving a word at a
   time (62 ms a word, 40 ms more after punctuation). The line is rewritten when the weather changes and knows the
   day ("Rain in 1 h 27 min. The umbrella is by the door." / "Rain until about midnight. Take the umbrella to the
   gym at 6:30." / "Cold tonight, 51. Bring the basil in before bed.").
3. The number is a dial: swipe across the glass and the temperature rolls through the next hours with the hour and a
   word under it; left alone it peeks at +2 h every ten seconds for three seconds and returns to now. Right side: the
   temperature it ends at by +5 h.
4. The sun's day: a 306 pt hairline arc is the sun's path from 7:25 to 6:06 with a bright point where it is now (amber
   in the last hour before sunset), the moon as a smaller point on its own path, rain hours as a dotted stretch of
   the arc; the temperature under it at 72 pt. The same grammar as a routine's life line.

The glass's own weather, in all four: condensation beads form and run on the inside on rain, fog and storm (up to 90
beads, each a bright spot with a dark lower edge and a highlight; large ones run and leave a fading trail); frost
grows from the four corners in a cold snap and melts when it clears; a soft flare slides across every 14 s in clear
sun. All inside the approved surface; the sky through the glass stays untouched otherwise.

Recommendation: 1 (plain numbers) as the base, with 2 (Tess's line) replacing the "feels like" line when she has
something to say, and the glass weather in all cases. 3 and 4 are good second pages of the slot if he wants the
slot to turn (a swipe up).

## His idea, Oct 25: THE SKYPIECE (direction, approved to pursue)
His words, kept close: "Let's call this the skypiece. It is a surface with depth, open upwards, and in that region
things can get in. When there is rain, drops stick on and water collects at the bottom. When there is snow, snow
collects at the bottom. In a thunderstorm, some places could have marks of burnt or lightning-stricken things. In
windy times the numbers shake a little. For special occasions, a Christmas theme around the numbers and so on;
that would be Tess doing those things. Even if it costs more to get these effects done, whatever tools we need, we
should get it done. If we figure out this and the background, we are perfect."
So: the keystone is an OPEN VESSEL. The sky is behind it; weather enters it and accumulates: water with a level and
a surface that ripples when drops land and sloshes with tilt, evaporating slowly in sun; snow in drifts along the
bottom and in the corners, melting when it warms; scorch marks from lightning that fade over days; everything
inside shaking in wind; seasonal dressing by Tess (lights, snowflakes) on special days. The numbers live in it.
Costs are accepted: whatever rendering is needed (Metal shaders, particle systems, a plate library) is in scope.
Built as direction 5 on the board, v2.
