The sink. Honest and a little funny: the keystone is the kitchen sink, and the dishes pile up in it as you cook and eat. Breakfast leaves two, dinner leaves five; a pot sticks out. The tap drips when the kitchen is quiet and a bubble pops now and then. Swipe right across the sink, or the Dishes word at the foot, and the tap runs, the sink fills with suds, and the dishes move one by one to the rack to dry. Tess keeps the count in the brief sentence. Nothing edible is drawn, only crockery, water and steel; and the sink tells you the one kitchen truth you asked to see.

The ticket rail. Every working kitchen has a pass with tickets clipped to a rail, and that is the keystone: the kitchen’s own paper. Tess writes a ticket for whatever the kitchen needs from you: Tacos, 25 min, tonight. Get limes, cilantro, tortillas. Rice goes out today. Dishes, seven waiting. The tickets flutter in the hood’s draft, harder while you cook. Do a thing and its ticket is spiked: it jumps up onto the spike and the rest slide along. A new need slides in from the right with a clip. Tap a ticket to open it.

How they work

Blackboard integration.The board is drawn on a canvas that covers the whole page, under the type. Between y 180 and 420 the page color crosses to slate through a feathered edge about 36 pt deep, built from noise so it looks like chalk dust rather than a gradient; on Black the slate is a shade above the page and the dust is what you see. No rounded rect, no hairline, no shadow. The chalk hand (Caveat) writes everything on it, including the two facts that used to be type.
What the board says (three options under the phone).Plan: today’s session and time, the week’s days ticked, the bests small. Record: the bests large with dates, the streak as weeks ticked, today’s tally. Coach: one sentence in Tess’s hand (“Bench opens at 185. Last time, three fives.”) with the week ticked under it.
The sink.Dishes are plates (ellipses with a rim), bowls, mugs and a pot, stacked with a little random tilt, the count a real number from meals cooked and eaten. Idle: a drip every 4 to 7 s with a ring on the water. Doing the dishes: the tap runs for 6 s, suds rise as 60 bubbles, dishes leave one by one (0.35 s apart) to the rack at the right, the water drains with a swirl. Swipe right = done, with a receipt and Undo. The Dishes word at the foot does the same and shows the count.
The rail.A steel rail across the top of the slot, tickets as 70 x 110 pt paper hanging from clips, Tess’s hand on them, a tear at the foot. Flutter is a slow sine per ticket, larger while cooking. Spike: the ticket rises 60 pt onto the spike at the right and fades; the rest slide left on a spring. New: a ticket slides in from beyond the right edge. Tap opens. The Dishes ticket carries the count and spikes when the dishes are done.
Dishes, in both kitchens.A first-class, frequent thing: a word at the foot beside Cook, a swipe on the sink, a ticket on the rail, and a count Tess keeps in the brief sentence. Done = receipt “Dishes, done.” with Undo 10 s; the count resets and grows again with meals.

Open, with a recommendation

Kitchen: sink or rail?

Both are kitchen material and both carry Dishes. The sink is the one living thing you look at every day and it has the better joke; the rail carries more information. Recommendation: the sink as the keystone, and the rail’s tickets as the grammar for Tess’s kitchen notes on the Tonight page.

Blackboard: how dark on Paper?

The slate on a paper page is a strong dark band. Recommendation: keep it, since a blackboard is dark, but let the dust feather run deeper (36 pt) so it never reads as a block.
