Routines, made of repetition.

You called the woodland the weakest keystone, and I think the reason is that it is a picture of habits rather than the stuff of them. Chalk came from the gym, tickets from the kitchen, the sky from the weather. Routines are made of repetition: a thing done again and again until it leaves a mark. Three keystones built from that, each in the standard slot, each able to stay through the app, each with a kinder answer to missing a day than withering. Press the events.

Worn paths. A field seen from above. Every routine is a path across it, worn by walking: the more days you keep it, the clearer and wider the path, down to bare earth for the old ones. Miss a while and the grass starts to grow back over it; it never vanishes, it just softens, and the next walk reopens it. When you do a routine, a small figure walks its path end to end in three seconds. Tap a path to open the routine. Behind a door, the field tilts to show only that grove’s paths. Paths are the kindest record there is: nothing dies, grass just grows.

The sampler. A piece of linen being embroidered, one row a day, one stitch per routine in its grove’s color. Done is a cross stitch; a planned rest is a small knot; a day not done is bare cloth, and bare cloth is just cloth, not a wound. Over weeks a pattern appears that is yours alone: the Tuesday you always skip, the month everything held. When you finish a routine the needle comes through and pulls the thread tight in a second and a half. Scroll the cloth back through the months with a finger. Behind a door, only that grove’s threads stay in color.

The string of beads. A counting string, the oldest way people kept a repetition. Today’s routines are the beads; you move one across the knot when it is done, and it clicks home with a haptic you can feel. Done beads sit on the right, glossy; the rest wait on the left, matte, in their grove’s color. Each full day ties a small knot at the end, so the string carries the month as a row of knots. A routine left for days goes dull, never red. The string hangs with real weight and sways when you tilt the phone. Behind a door, the string shows only that grove’s beads.

How they work

All three keep the 248 landing.Title, the three-colored sentence, the keystone in the standard slot, the two updates, and the doors. Only the keystone changes; the Sheaf stays behind the doors (approved).
All three stay through the app.Behind a door the keystone filters to that grove (paths tilt, threads stay colored, beads thin out). On a routine’s page it focuses: its one path, its one thread’s column, its one bead large.
Missing a day, three kindnesses.Paths: grass grows back a little, nothing dies. Sampler: a bare stitch is just cloth. Beads: a bead goes dull. None uses red, none counts a streak out loud, and a planned rest is honored in each (a path kept clear by a marker, a knot stitch, a bead set aside).
What they show without a number.Paths: how established each habit is (width and clarity) and which are fading. Sampler: the shape of your weeks and months at a glance, with the pattern as the reward. Beads: how today is going (left vs right) and how many full days this month (knots).
Reactions, all under five seconds.Paths: a figure walks the path (3 s). Sampler: the needle pulls the stitch (1.5 s). Beads: a bead slides and clicks (0.6 s), a knot ties at day’s end (2 s). New routine: a faint new path, a new thread color, a new bead on the left.
Material.Paths: a real grass plate from above (procedural here), paths as worn earth. Sampler: real linen texture (procedural here), thread as two-tone stitches. Beads: wood or stone beads with a highlight, a cotton string that bows.

Where I would start

The sampler. It is the only one that gets more beautiful the longer you use it, it carries real information (your weeks as a pattern) without a chart, it scales to years, and bare cloth is the gentlest possible way to show a day not done. The beads are the best to touch and the best haptic in the app; they could be the Today door’s keystone rather than the landing’s. The paths are the loveliest idea and the hardest to make look real.
