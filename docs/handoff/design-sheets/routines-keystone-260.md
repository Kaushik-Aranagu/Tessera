# 260 Routines keystone, round two: made of repetition (v1, Oct 25; not yet reviewed)

Board: https://claude.ai/artifact/RMjtTWuT4bE6MVmWEb6KNc (file `routines-keystone-260/board.html`). He called the woodland (248) the
weakest keystone and asked for a redesign with new ideation. Reading of why: the woodland is a picture of habits (a
scene with a caretaker, the habit garden every app has) rather than the stuff of them; chalk came from the gym,
tickets from the kitchen, the sky from the weather. Routines' material is REPETITION: a thing done again and again
until it leaves a mark. Three keystones from that, each in the standard slot, each able to stay through the app,
each with a kinder answer to a missed day than withering. The 248 landing around the slot is kept (title, the
three-colored sentence, two updates, doors); the Sheaf behind the doors is approved and unchanged.

## A. Worn paths
A field from above (a real grass plate on the phone; procedural here). Every routine is a path across it, worn by
walking: days kept set width and clarity (2 to 11 pt, bare earth for the old ones). Days since the last walk let the
grass creep back (tufts over the path, width and alpha fall); nothing ever vanishes, and the next walk reopens it.
Doing a routine: a small figure in the grove's color walks its path end to end in 3 s. Tap a path to open the
routine. Behind a door the field shows only that grove's paths; on a routine's page, its one path.
## B. The sampler
A piece of linen (real linen texture on the phone) embroidered one ROW A DAY, one STITCH PER ROUTINE in its grove's
color (Body teal, Mind plum, Home clay, Across the lake slate). Done is a cross stitch; a planned rest is a small
knot; a day not done is bare cloth, and bare cloth is only cloth. Mondays have a longer tick at the left edge. Rows
run newest at the bottom; drag the cloth down to scroll back through the months. Finishing a routine: the needle
comes through and pulls the stitch tight in 1.5 s. Tap a column to open that routine (its thread). Behind a door only
that grove's threads stay in color; on a routine's page, its one column, long.
## C. The string of beads
A counting string: today's routines are the beads, waiting on the left (matte, grove color), done on the right of a
knot (glossy, with a highlight). Swipe a bead right across the knot and it slides and CLICKS home (the best haptic in
the app); swipe left to take it back. A routine left for three or more days goes dull, never red. Each full day ties
a knot along the bottom, so the string carries the month as a row of knots, with a tassel at the end. The string
bows under the beads and tilts with a drag (and the phone's motion). Behind a door, only that grove's beads.

## Rules held
Standard slot (354 x 182 at 196); the centerpiece stays through the app; no red, no streak counted out loud; a
planned rest honored in each; reactions under 5 s; the approved soft edge (hairline of light and shade) on all three.

## Recommendation
The sampler: it gets more beautiful with use, carries the shape of your weeks without a chart, scales to years, and
bare cloth is the gentlest way to show a day not done. The beads are the best to touch; they could be the Today
door's keystone instead of the landing's. The paths are the loveliest idea and the hardest to make look real.

## Verdict (Kaushik, Oct 25): none of the three. The sampler is "a little bit nice" but "doesn't have enough to say
it's good". Another round is needed. Open question put to him before round three: should the Routines keystone be
about the DAY (what is next, where in the day you are, the rhythm) or about the LONG RUN (how habits hold over
weeks and months)? Paths, sampler and beads were all long-run pieces; the woodland was too.

## His steer for round three (Oct 25, his words kept close)
"It could be anything. I'm just looking for creative ideas, because at the end of the day it's about having some
fun. If I can have some relation to it, that's the cherry on top. I'm trying to add more layers: once I have
something relevant, Tess or the AI layer can take care of how it reacts, and it can have its own life. I'm looking
for things which can have life, or seem to have life, show some expressiveness, show something, and be unique and
fun." Not day vs long run, then: LIFE, EXPRESSIVENESS, FUN, UNIQUENESS, and a personal relation if possible; a
creature or world Tess can animate from the routine data.
