How it works

1Routines, the landing, is a summary. Three plain lines about today: what's done and what's left, what's late or next, and the week's rhythm in words. Then doors, one line of fact under each: Today (the day's routines in order), then the kinds: Mornings, Evenings, Body (warm-ups, mobility and the rower, built from Training's moves), Home (chores and resets), Mind (reading, journaling, breathing) and Borrowed (routines that live in another room and stay linked to it). 2New routine is the action word. Tap it to make one: name it, pick its kind, then add steps or keep it one thing. Drag it left for One thing (straight to the short version), right for From another room: a Training warm-up, a Kitchen prep or a Lists checklist, its room named in that room's color. 3A kind of routine. Its routines on the depth wheel. A one-thing routine reads as one line, with no step count ("Water the plants"); a routine with steps shows how many and about how long. Tap a row to bring it to the front; tap the front one to open it. 4Today. The week as letters (a day with every routine kept is plum, today is a size larger), the rhythm in words, and the day's routines by time. A late one shows its time in wine. Start opens the front routine; drag left for Skip today, right for Later, 9 pm. 5A routine on its own page, as 238 v2: the rhythm in words, the last 14 days as marks all one length (plum and heavier for done, light ink3 for missed, no count), and its steps. Tap or swipe right to check a step (a thin plum line, 1.5 s, then it drops to Done); swipe left for Not now. Start, then Next step; drag left for Skip today ("Skipped today."), right for Restart ("Evening stretch, restarted."). Undo for 10 s on everything. 6Going back: swipe in from the left edge (28 pt) or press Esc. Every tap target is at least 44 pt. Reduce Motion swaps pages and wheels in place. 7Privacy. A private routine reads normally here and as "Private routine" off the phone (Lock Screen, widgets, Tess and Claude lines, backups). Borrowed routines keep their home room's privacy.
