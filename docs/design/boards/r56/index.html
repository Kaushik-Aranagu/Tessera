Room landings, one composed page

His 11:39, 11:45 and 11:52 PM: a landing is not a list or stacked sections. It's a short summary with a few ways in. Here are three systems, each drawn for Routines and Kitchen. Every coloured word or label is a door, so tap it to open the page behind it. Each page uses 3 or 4 colours, each with a meaning.

A The paragraph

The room speaks in one paragraph. It's set at 27 pt, like the Home board, and reads top to bottom as a summary. The bold words in their role colours are the doors. There are no labels, no sections and no list. The next thing opens it, and the room's parts sit inside the prose, so the whole page is one thought. Other apps draw on the same idea: Journal's prompts, Weather's sentence summary and Fitness's "Move 420 of 500".

One fine-line drawing fills the upper page, and its regions are the doors. Each part of the room has a place in the drawing with its name and one number beside it. C The contents page

A magazine contents page. One sentence of state sits up top. Below it, a handful of big words are set at different sizes by how much they matter today. Each word has one quiet fact beside it, and that's the only text. There are no headers or groups, and the words are the doors. The system, whichever is picked

Every room landing has the same parts. First, a title with the weekday. Second, one statement of now: the next thing, or what needs you. Third, three to six doors into the room's parts, each with one fact. Last, the action word bottom-left with tap and drag options, and Tess bottom-right. Each page has 3 or 4 colours, each with a meaning: the room accent for the room and what's next, wine for late or going out, and one or two role colours for its parts (gold, green, slate, as in the Library). A door is never a list row, a section header or a card. Recommendation: A, the paragraph. It reads like Home and Tess, and it's the calmest. It also works for every room, including Money ("About 1,240 left this month; rent goes out Monday") and Notes.
